Hinges are the hardest-working hardware on any hinged aluminium window or door, and around Wellington they cop the worst of the weather. Salt air off the coast gets into the pivots and fixings, and sooner or later you see what's in the photo below: a powder-coated hinge blooming white with corrosion. Once it gets to that stage, lubricating it is a temporary fix at best — the hinge needs replacing.

## What corroded hinges do to your joinery

A rusty hinge rarely fails on its own. As the pivots seize and the plates corrode, the sash or door starts to work against it:

- The sash **drops or sits crooked**, so it no longer lines up with the catch — you push harder on the handle, and the handle or lock wears out next.
- The seals stop seating evenly, letting in **draughts and water** that the joinery used to keep out.
- In a good Wellington northerly, a seized hinge puts all the load on the fixings — and that's how screws strip and sashes end up flapping.

Caught early, this is a straightforward, inexpensive repair. Left alone, one rusty hinge can take the handle, the seals and the fixings with it.

## Why hinge replacement is a matching job

Here's the part that catches people out: **not every hinge — or hinge placement — gives the same result.** Aluminium joinery systems have been made by many fabricators over the decades, and each used its own hinges: different plate sizes, hole patterns, pivot offsets, handing and load ratings. Two hinges that look almost identical on the shelf can hang the same sash quite differently.

Placement matters just as much. The original fabricator positioned each hinge for that sash's weight and geometry. Refit a new hinge even a few millimetres off, or choose one with a different pivot offset, and the sash sits differently in the frame — it binds, won't latch cleanly, or holds the seals off the frame so the draughts and leaks stay. And because it's now carrying load unevenly, the new hinge wears out early too.

That's why the tape measure comes out before anything is unscrewed. We identify the joinery system, measure the hinge and its position, and match a replacement that hangs the sash the way it was designed to — using the existing fixing points where they're sound, and re-aligning the sash so it latches and seals properly. Where the environment calls for it, we fit stainless or coastal-grade hardware so the new hinges outlast the old ones.
